## Executive Decision

**NO-GO for production notes-enabled reservation or auto-approval.** The checked-in backend currently exposes a direct authorization bypass: `POST /api/parts/{sku}/reservations` accepts calls without session token, confirmation, job ownership, or job-status checks, and its service decrements part total quantity without creating a job-linked reservation. The MCP reserve handler also forwards without session authorization. The backend therefore violates relevant Feature 2 requirements for ownership and job-linked reservation/inventory integrity, and conflicts with the separate MCP contracts' session, dispatch-brief, and confirmation controls. The latter controls are documented in `tool-design.md` and `tools/parts-stock-tool.md`, not imposed by `requirements.md`. This is established behavior, not merely a control gap of unknown status. The current MCP artifacts and checked-in implementation lack a canonical trusted confirmation/policy authorization contract; the new security design proposes the recommended contract, which remains unimplemented and requires supporting verification. The proposal additionally lacks job notes and secure notes retrieval. Untrusted notes could influence an LLM; deterministic authorization must therefore remain outside the model and must not derive authority from notes. Do not enable notes-enabled automated writes or auto-approval until every release condition below is met and independently evidenced.

This is a design-stage assessment, not a penetration test, compliance opinion, or claim that mitigations are operating. Ratings estimate the proposed system if the stated controls are implemented; residual ratings are conditional estimates, not evidence of completed risk treatment.

## Scope, Method, and Assumptions

Assessment covers note storage and retrieval, prompt-injection exposure, session and job authorization, MCP tools, reservation integrity, outputs/logging, operational failure, and privacy risk. Method: qualitative threat scenarios grounded in Feature 2 requirements (for ownership/status and inventory integrity), the separate MCP contracts (for session token, dispatch brief, and human confirmation), related designs, current `Job` model, and checked-in MCP handler; likelihood and impact are scored on the scales below, and mitigations are mapped to proposed evidence and accountable roles.

Assumptions and boundaries:

- Proposed `jobNotes` is free text, can originate from technicians, dispatchers, customers, or copied/imported material, and is not trusted based on author label.
- Parts reservation changes shared stock and is consequential. `requirements.md` requires issuing against a technician's own `SCHEDULED` job and defines job-linked issue/return and inventory-integrity behavior. Separately, `tool-design.md` and `tools/parts-stock-tool.md` define session-token and dispatch-brief controls, and the MCP workflow requires explicit human confirmation. The checked-in backend does not enforce the applicable ownership/job-linkage requirements or the documented MCP controls on its reservation route.
- No production identity/session provider, deployed model configuration, persistent production datastore, data classification, retention schedule, jurisdiction, or operational monitoring evidence was supplied. No evidence is inferred from design prose.
- Current `Job` has no notes field. Current MCP source contains a placeholder session brief; its reserve handler does not validate `meta.session_token` or confirmation and forwards to the backend without a token. The backend implementation is established by inspection: `PartController` does not require authorization/context or validate job ownership/status, and `PartService.reserve` directly subtracts from `Part.totalQuantity` without storing a job-linked reservation. Direct API calls can therefore bypass MCP checks and mutate stock.
- Confirmation terminology is inconsistent across the MCP artifacts: `tool-design.md` calls the brief field `humanConfirmationState`, while `tools/parts-stock-tool.md` and the checked-in placeholder use `humanConfirmed` (the placeholder sets it to `false`). Neither a boolean nor a model-produced assertion authenticates the actor or binds an approval to an exact reservation tuple. `requirements.md` does not prescribe session tokens, dispatch briefs, or human confirmation.
- No legal or regulatory compliance determination is made. Privacy/legal review is mandatory before personal or customer data is introduced into model context or persisted under this proposal.

## Rating Scale

**Likelihood (L):** 1 Rare (exceptional circumstances); 2 Unlikely; 3 Possible; 4 Likely; 5 Almost certain under expected exposure.

**Impact (I):** 1 Negligible; 2 Minor/local; 3 Moderate operational or limited data exposure; 4 Major financial, privacy, or service impact; 5 Severe, broad, or difficult-to-reverse impact.

**Rating:** score = L × I. Low 1–4; Moderate 5–9; High 10–16; Critical 17–25. Inherent is before proposed controls. Residual assumes the stated controls are implemented effectively, independently tested, monitored, and kept current. Current implementation status is separately identified and does not reduce the inherent score.

Residual ratings are conditional estimates only, after the listed controls are implemented and evidenced; they do not describe the current baseline. Until backend authorization is fixed and the direct-call state-preservation tests pass, the actual bypass and reservation-integrity risks remain Critical and untreated. Even after remediation, residual risk remains High for backend authorization bypass impact and for confirmation/auto-policy misuse because a successful exploit can still cause consequential inventory changes. Prompt-injection risk remains at least High because model interpretation of hostile natural language cannot be made perfectly reliable; the treatment is to prevent that interpretation from carrying authority, not to claim injection can be eliminated.

## Current Evidence and Gaps

- [backend/src/main/java/com/serviceforge/model/Job.java](../../../backend/src/main/java/com/serviceforge/model/Job.java) contains identity, schedule, status, and `requiredSkill`; it has no notes field.
- [pipeline/feature2/requirements.md](../requirements.md) requires a technician's own `SCHEDULED` job and describes job-linked issue/return and inventory-integrity behavior; it does not require a session token, dispatch brief, or human confirmation. [pipeline/feature2/tool-design.md](../tool-design.md) and [pipeline/feature2/tools/parts-stock-tool.md](../tools/parts-stock-tool.md) separately document the session/dispatch-brief and confirmation controls, but use inconsistent field names (`humanConfirmationState` and `humanConfirmed`). The checked-in placeholder contains `humanConfirmed: false` and does not constitute authenticated confirmation.
- [backend/src/main/java/com/serviceforge/controller/PartController.java](../../../backend/src/main/java/com/serviceforge/controller/PartController.java) accepts direct reservation requests with quantity and optional `jobId`; it does not require session/context, confirmation, ownership, or job status. [backend/src/main/java/com/serviceforge/service/PartService.java](../../../backend/src/main/java/com/serviceforge/service/PartService.java) subtracts from total quantity and does not create a job-linked reservation. These checked-in source observations establish the bypass and conflict with the Feature 2 requirements and documented MCP contract; they are not a claim about every deployed runtime.
- [pipeline/feature2/mcp_server/server.py](../mcp_server/server.py) includes a placeholder `dispatch-brief` resource and its reserve handler forwards a backend request without enforcing a session token or human-confirmation state and without passing a token to the backend. MCP-side enforcement alone would not prevent direct API calls.
- [pipeline/feature2/architecture-adr.md](../architecture-adr.md) and [pipeline/feature2/adr-review.md](../adr-review.md) describe the proposed inventory design and review findings; they are design artifacts, not proof of implemented controls.
- [pipeline/feature2/memory-design.md](../memory-design.md) and [pipeline/feature2/rules/memory-retention-rule.md](../rules/memory-retention-rule.md) provide memory retention intentions, but do not establish model-provider prompt retention, application log redaction, or job-notes lifecycle controls.
- No notes endpoint, notes-specific validation, production identity binding, auto-approval policy, or test/evidence package for the proposed flow was established in the reviewed sources.

## Go-Live Conditions

NO-GO remains until all conditions are met and evidence is reviewed:

1. Close the verified direct backend bypass before notes-enabled automated writes: all reservation routes, including direct `POST /api/parts/{sku}/reservations`, must enforce the `requirements.md` ownership/status and job-linked inventory-integrity behavior, and reject calls lacking the trusted session/authorization context documented by the MCP contract. The backend is authoritative for confirmation/policy validation. Integration tests must call the backend directly without authorized context and prove rejection with part totals, job state, and reservation records unchanged; repeat through MCP. MCP validation alone does not satisfy this gate.
2. Before release, reconcile `humanConfirmationState` and `humanConfirmed` in the inconsistent MCP documents to the recommended canonical trusted structured confirmation record: authenticated issuer and actor, exact `(jobId, sku, quantity)` tuple bound to session/technician, expiry, and unique one-time replay-safe identifier. The authoritative backend must validate and atomically consume it. Reject booleans and model output as confirmation. Any auto-approval design must use a distinct deterministic server-issued grant bound to the exact tuple, authenticated principal, policy version, short expiry, and one-time identifier; bound eligibility to an allowlisted role, owned `SCHEDULED` job, allowlisted SKU, quantity cap, and authoritative peak-window stock/minimum-stock conditions. Notes/model output cannot grant approval or modify conditions. Keep auto mode disabled until separately approved and verified.
3. Approve the Job notes data scope, entry permissions, validation/size limit, retention and deletion, backups, model-provider processing, and access roles through privacy/legal and security review.
4. Implement the minimal authenticated notes projection and prove cross-job/unauthorized reads are rejected; do not expose notes to the model before this gate.
5. Add strict notes/tool isolation and deterministic output-schema enforcement; pass the adversarial suite without unauthorized tool calls, reservation, or sensitive-data disclosure.
6. Demonstrate atomic job-linked reservation, job/status/stock checks, idempotency, stale-state handling, fail-closed behavior, and complete but minimized audit events in automated integration tests.
7. Keep auto-approval disabled until a business owner approves explicit eligibility rules and a separate security review signs the deployed policy, monitoring, pilot evidence, and rollback drill.
8. Obtain named sign-off from Security, Product/Inventory Operations, Platform/SRE, and Privacy/Legal before production exposure. Any unresolved high/critical risk or missing evidence means NO-GO.

## Review and Approval Record

No implementation evidence or reviewer approval is recorded in this draft. Proposed accountable roles above are not named individuals. Privacy/legal review is required and pending. Reassess after implementation, threat-model changes, model/provider changes, new note sources, or any expansion of tool privileges.
